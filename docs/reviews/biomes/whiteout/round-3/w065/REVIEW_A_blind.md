# Reviewer A (Art & Visual Readability): blind review, w065 round 3

Source: the 16 screenshots in `blind/` (view01 to view08, X and Y). I read no code, history or other documents.

In all 8 pairs the right side of the frame is the same in X and Y: a rising, rock-studded mountainside with scattered trees, plus the course markers ahead. Every difference between the builds is on the **left side of the frame**: what the left skyline looks like and what lies beyond the left edge of the piste. So below I mostly describe the left side.

---

## view01 (clear; caption "the storm eases, the view opens up")

**X:** On the right is a steep rocky slope with boulders near the top. On the left the near snow rises gently to a soft rim at about y≈330 to 400, with a few small conifers on it. Past that rim the near ground stops and there is a broad, flat, pale grey-blue expanse (y≈290 to 400) stretching to a far, low, jagged skyline at the left edge (y≈150 to 230). There is a lot of sky. The way the near ground ends at a rim with a much lower, distant plain beyond it suggests the ground falls away to the **left / left of centre**. But you never see the drop itself: no cliff face, no rock band, no dark edge. The expanse has streaky, cloud-like texture, so it reads about as much like a **cloud sea or fog bank** as like a valley floor far below.

**Y:** The near ground on the left is the same, but behind it a tall, pale, flat-shaded ridge climbs from the left edge (y≈50) down toward the centre. It is a closed valley: walls on both sides and a V-shaped notch of sky straight ahead. There is no sense of a drop.

**Comparison:** **X** is clearly more open and gives more sense of height on the left. Y reads as a sheltered valley. Even in X, though, "exposed" is something you infer from the far horizon, not a drop you can see.

**Reads wrong / confusing:** A large white glow/spray sprite covers the skier in both images, so you can barely see the character. The flat pale left ridge in Y has almost no shading and looks like a backdrop card. In X the left expanse could be cloud.

## view02 (clear)

**X:** The right is the same steep rocky slope. On the left, the near piste runs out to a low lip with rocks on it (y≈390 to 420). Beyond the lip is a wide, flat, misty expanse with streaky bands, then a distant low serrated skyline (y≈140 to 240). Straight ahead, a soft grey fog "curtain" fills the gap between the expanse and the right slope. The left reads as **open and dropping away**, mid-left of frame. Again the only cues are the low far horizon and the rim. There is no edge geometry or rock face.

**Y:** A high pale ridge fills the upper left (from y≈60 at the left edge down to the centre notch). The left foreground has low rocky mounds. It is an enclosed valley.

**Comparison:** **X** is more open. Same pattern as view01.

**Reads wrong / confusing:** In X the flat expanse on the left could be read as more snowfield you can ski onto, or as cloud. Nothing marks it as off-piste or as a drop. The fog wedge ahead hides part of the route beyond the orange markers, but the markers and the trail line stay visible, so you can still read the route.

## view06 (clear)

## Overall summary

**Which image looked more open or exposed, per view:**

| View | Weather | More exposed | Strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | clear | X | clear difference |
| 02 | clear | X | clear difference |
| 03 | storm | equivalent | none |
| 04 | storm | X (barely) | negligible |
| 05 | clear | equivalent (Y slightly more sky) | negligible |
| 06 | clear | Y | clear difference |
| 07 | storm | equivalent | none |
| 08 | storm | Y (barely) | faint |

**Is one build consistent?** Yes, as far as I can tell. One build keeps opening the left side: the near ground ends at a rim and you see a low, far horizon with a flat pale expanse below it. The other build has a tall pale ridge on the left that walls in the valley. The letters swap between pairs, but the pattern is the same: the open-left build is X in 01, 02 and probably 04, and Y in 06 and 08. In views 05 and 07 both builds are walled in or fogged, so they look the same.

**Is it "immediately recognizable" as dramatic exposure?** **Partly visible, not immediately recognizable.** In the clear views (01, 02, 06) the open-left build obviously looks **more open and higher up**. It reads as "I'm on a shoulder above a big valley" rather than "I'm in a trough". But it does **not** read as a dramatic drop, cliff or void:
- You never see the drop itself: no cliff face, no rock band, no dark shadowed edge, no cornice, no near-vertical surface falling away below the rim.
- The ground beyond the rim is a flat, pale, streaky plane, close in value to the sky and the near snow. It reads as a cloud sea, a far snowfield or haze about as easily as a valley far below.
- The camera is low and pitched toward the slope, so the rim cuts across the frame at about the same height as the horizon. You never look down over the edge.

So a player would probably think "the view opened up" (which matches the caption), but most would not think "there's a deadly drop to my left".

**Does either build hide the route or hazards?** Neither build hides the course markers or the trail line in any view, and the right-side hazards (rocks, trees) are the same in both. In the open build, nothing marks the open left rim as dangerous or off-limits (no fence, flags, rope or dark edge). If falling off is meant to be possible, the hazard is under-communicated. If it is only scenery, it could tempt players to ski toward what looks like open snowfield. Separately from the builds, the curling grey "wave" fog bank in view05 (both builds) hides the course beyond the bend and looks like a solid object. And the white spray glow over the skier in 01 and 03 (both builds) hides the character.

**Storm views (03, 04, 07, 08):** The two builds look effectively the same in a storm. The fog flattens everything to a low-contrast grey-white, sky and ground nearly merge, and the only depth cues are the nearest trees, poles and orange markers. You can read the route (markers and tracks stay visible) and you can see the right-hand slope. You cannot read any exposure, edge or drop in either build. A faint left-edge difference exists (08, and barely 04), but it would not register while playing. If the drop is supposed to be felt in a storm, neither build does it.

**Suggestions (art direction, not from code):** To make the exposure read, the open build needs visible edge geometry at the rim: a darker rock lip or cornice, a band of falling rock face just below the rim line, or a few treetops or rocks seen *below* the rim at a smaller scale. The expanse beyond needs stronger aerial perspective that is clearly *below* (darker or bluer valley floor, rather than a flat pale plane that matches the clouds). In storm conditions, a darker silhouette of the drop edge, or edge markers such as poles or rope, would be the only way to keep that hazard readable.
