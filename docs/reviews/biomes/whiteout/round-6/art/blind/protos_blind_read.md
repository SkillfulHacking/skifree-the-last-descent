# Blind read: variants W, X, Y, Z (24 screenshots)

All four variants share the same base. On the right is a big snow-and-scree ridge wall that rises steeply. Down the middle is a wide groomed corridor with thin orange marker poles and ski tracks. On the left the snow rolls off into something lower and far away. The storm frames are almost the same in every variant: flat white-grey fog, a lighter hill on the right, glowing pole tips, and a caption saying the wind pushes you left. Almost all of the difference between the variants is on the left horizon in the clear frames.

---

## W

1. **Drop-off or edge**
   - Clear: yes, on the left. The snow rim ends and a flat grey surface lies beyond it, lower and farther away. Spot 1 also shows a tree-lined shelf stepping down on the left. Confidence 6/10. The far surface comes up almost to the height of the snow horizon, so it reads as much like a flat plain level with you as like a drop.
   - Storm: no visible edge. The left is a soft white roll of snow. Spot 2 has a frosted rock at the far left that faintly hints at a rim. The caption "wind pushes you left" suggests danger on the left, but the picture doesn't show it. Confidence 2/10.
2. **Safe route visible?** Clear 7/10: orange poles run down the centre, especially at spot 2. Storm 5/10: the glowing pole tips and the ski tracks carry the route.
3. **Beyond the edge:** a flat, grainy, blue-grey plane with dark wavy streaks, like a frozen sea, a sheet of ice or a cloud deck seen from above. A pale ghostly mountain range sits on its horizon. It reads as water or ice more than as a valley.
4. **Rock formations:** one dark angular rock block at spot 2, upper left (about x 120–210, y 190–240), plus a smaller rock beside it. It reads as one mid-sized boulder or tor, a prop more than a formation. Otherwise the scree on the right wall is small scattered prop rocks.
5. **Fake or backdrop:**
   - Vertical white plumes rise from the left rim at spot 0 (x 60–380) and spot 2 (x 220–270). They look like billboard sprites or steam columns, not blowing snow.
   - The left plane's horizon sits nearly level with the snow lip, so it can read as a continuous flat surface you could ski onto.
   - Top right of every clear frame: a pale, sawtooth ridge silhouette (x 600–960, y 0–200) reads as a flat cutout card.
6. **Distinctiveness:** 5/10.

## X

1. **Drop-off or edge**
   - Clear: yes, on the left. Spot 1 has the clearest single edge cue of the set: a crisp dark lip line runs diagonally from about (80, 325) to (180, 230), plainly a cliff or cornice edge with the grey plane far below. At spot 0 the plane starts right behind the snow rim (about y 225), which weakens the cue. Spot 2 has no rock marking the rim. Confidence 6/10.
   - Storm: none visible; the frames are effectively the same as W's. Confidence 2/10. Spot 2 storm doesn't even have W's frosted rock.
2. **Safe route visible?** Clear 7/10, storm 5/10, the same as W.
3. **Beyond the edge:** the same flat grey sea of ice or cloud as W. It sits a little higher and hazier at spot 0, so it looks closer.
4. **Rock formations:** none of note on the left. Only small scattered scree and boulders on the right wall, all props.
5. **Fake or backdrop:**
   - At spot 0 the left plane meets the snow almost seamlessly at about y 220–230, which lies about where you can ski: it looks like a flat continuation.
   - The same sprite-like vertical plumes on the left rim and the same cutout ridge at top right as W.
6. **Distinctiveness:** 4/10, the most generic of the four.

## Y

1. **Drop-off or edge**
   - Clear: probably on the left, but it's much less clear. There is no hard surface beyond the rim, only a bright milky haze. Spot 1 shows a tree line descending to the left into a white void, and spot 0 shows a pale wall of haze with vertical streaks. It could be a foggy valley, or it could be a cloud bank sitting on more slope. Confidence 4/10.
   - Storm: none visible. Spot 2 has a faint grey rock outline at the far left. Confidence 2/10.
2. **Safe route visible?** Clear 6/10. The poles are visible, but the bright haze on the left makes the whole left half low-contrast, so the corridor has no firm left boundary. Storm 5/10.
3. **Beyond the edge:** cloud or fog: a bright white-blue haze, maybe a fog-filled valley. There's no water or ice reading. At spot 2, dark distant peaks appear through the haze.
4. **Rock formations:** at spot 2, two dark triangular peaks on the left (x 30–220) and several small rock humps in the middle distance (x 340–460). They read as distant mountain tops rather than spires at the edge, so they suggest more land, not a drop. They look natural more than prop-like.
5. **Fake or backdrop:**
   - At spot 0 the haze on the left (x 0–440, y 160–290) has hard vertical streaks, like a painted curtain or wall.
   - At spot 1 the left (x 0–260, y 180–330) is a near-white flat void with no depth cues.
   - The cutout ridge at top right, as in the others.
   - Because the haze looks soft and continuous with the snow, it doesn't tell you the left is out of bounds. It understates the danger.
6. **Distinctiveness:** 5/10. The haze is moody, but it reads as weather, not as a place.

## Z

1. **Drop-off or edge**
   - Clear: yes, on the left, and the most legible of the four.
     - Spot 0: a grey rock outcrop sits at the left rim (x 170–260) with the flat grey plane below and beyond it.
     - Spot 1: a tree-lined lip descends left to a lower shelf.
     - Spot 2: a row of jagged dark rock fins runs along the left rim and recedes into the distance (x 0–500, y 185–255). It traces the edge line, so you can see where the edge goes ahead of you.
     - The right wall is darker and rockier, with dark bands. That raises contrast and frames the corridor between a rock wall on the right and a rock-lined rim on the left.
     - Confidence 8/10.
   - Storm: still mostly hidden. At spot 0 a large pale snow-covered rock mass shows at the far left (x 0–90, y 230–280), and at spot 2 a frosted white shape stands by the near pole. Both hint at "something on the left", but neither reads as a drop. Confidence 3/10.
2. **Safe route visible?** Clear 8/10: the line of spires on the left plus the dark wall on the right make the open corridor obvious, with poles down the middle. Storm 5/10.
3. **Beyond the edge:** the same flat grey sea of ice or cloud as W and X, with the ghost range on its horizon. It reads as a frozen sea or an ice shelf far below.
4. **Rock formations:** yes. At spot 2 there is a chain of sharp spires or fins along the rim, plus the spot 0 outcrop and a darker, more massive right wall at spots 0 and 1. The spire row reads as a natural ridge crest, partly. Each fin is a fairly simple low-poly wedge, and they repeat in size and shape, so up close they risk reading as copied props.
5. **Fake or backdrop:**
   - The same sprite-like vertical plumes on the left at spots 0 and 2 (x 60–380).
   - Storm spot 2: the white, sail-shaped frosted object at the near pole (x 140–210, y 200–250) looks like a flat paper cutout or flag, not a rock.
   - The flat grey plane still reaches nearly the height of the snow horizon, so the vertical size of the drop is undersold.
   - The cutout ridge at top right, as in the others.
6. **Distinctiveness:** 7/10. The spires and the dark rock wall give it a recognisable "knife-edge ridge above a frozen sea" identity.

## Ranking: "I can immediately tell where the dangerous edge is and where to ski, in clear and in storm"

1. **Z:** the rim is marked by rock outcrops and the spire chain, the right wall is high-contrast, and it has the only storm silhouettes that hint at the left side.
2. **X:** the crisp dark cliff lip at spot 1 is the best single edge read in the set. But spot 0 blends the plane into the slope, and spot 2 has nothing on the rim.
3. **W:** close to X. One boulder marks the rim at spot 2, but there is no crisp lip line, and the far plane reads as level ground.
4. **Y:** the haze erases the edge. The left reads as fog or more mountain, not as a drop, and the distant peaks suggest more terrain.

In storm all four are close to a tie (2–3/10 on the edge). The storm frames are almost the same across variants, and the danger is shown only by the caption and by the glowing poles, not by the scenery.

**Single biggest weakness of the winner (Z):** in the storm the edge disappears completely. None of the rock spires or lip cues that make Z work in clear weather survive the fog, so a player in a storm can't see the left-hand drop and has to rely on the caption and the pole lights. A second, smaller problem in clear weather: the far grey plane sits nearly level with the snow horizon, so even with the spires the drop reads shallow, like a flat sea next to you rather than a fall below you.
